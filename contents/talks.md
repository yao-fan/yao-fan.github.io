<p><strong>Abstract.</strong> A common line among Marxist political theorists is that justice is part of the ideology of capitalism, invented by the ruling class, to exploit and fool the educated bourgeoisie (the middle class). This idea originates from Karl Marx himself, who left several controversial and confusing remarks about justice in writing. At some places, Marx suggests that capitalism is robbery. At other places, he says that capitalism is just. Together he seems to be suggesting that robbery is just.</p>
<p>In this talk, I will explain what these controversial remarks are about. I will first introduce Marx's theory of exploitation (in its modern form) to make sense of the claim that "capitalism is robbery." I will explain why the theory of exploitation is not an adequate condemnation of capitalism and why Marx needs a theory of justice. Then I will move on to the discussion of justice, explaining senses in which capitalism is just and in which it is not. In a nutshell, capitalism can be transactionally just but structurally unjust.</p>
